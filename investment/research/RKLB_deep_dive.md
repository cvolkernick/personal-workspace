## Thesis fit

**Northstar:** Space economy lens, *in addition to* energy / Bitcoin / digital credit / AI / robotics. Maps to ~60% stocks/growth, **not** the 40% BTC complex.

**Why RKLB vs other space names:** it is the only public attempt at Falcon-class *launch economics*. ASTS is Starlink D2C (different stack). FLY is small-lift with a mixed Alpha record. LUNR/PL/RDW are not launch competitors.

**Why not instead of SPCX:** SpaceX already holds launch cadence, reuse at scale, and Starlink cash. RKLB is a **call option on a second Western medium-lift provider**, not a substitute.

## Risks (material)

1. **Neutron does not fly on time, or Flight 1 fails.** History of class-jumps (New Glenn, others) is ugly. Pre-building subsequent vehicles = stranded-capital risk if the design changes (SA 2026-08-24).
2. **Valuation assumes the option pays.** ~$40B on <$800M TTM sales with negative FCF.
3. **Iridium dilution + integration.** Subscription ops are a different company.
4. **SPCX overlap.** Book already has the winner. Space-beta doubles down.
5. **Customer concentration / USG.** NSSL is gated on flight. Commercial Neutron backlog is real but not Falcon-cadence.
6. **Capital.** Thin buffer. Overdue expenses exist. Do not spend residual on an unflown rocket.

## Critic

The phrase "relatively competitive with SpaceX" is doing too much work. Electron is not competitive with Falcon 9. Neutron is a slide. Buying RKLB at ~50× sales is paying SpaceX-competitor prices for a company whose competitive product does not exist yet. The −55% from ATH removes melt-up, not risk. If the goal is space exposure, **we already have it (SPCX)**. If the goal is a hedge against SpaceX monopoly, Neutron has to *work* — and even then Falcon/Starship cost/kg still wins on paper. Strongest use of scarce residual remains **energy gap (BE)** or **AI mega-cap (NVDA/GOOGL)**, not a second space name.
