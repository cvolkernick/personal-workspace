# Global Asset Allocation — latest public figures (compiled 17 Oct 2026)

**Can we show how global money/wealth is allocated?** Partly. Public sources give a decent *order-of-magnitude* picture of the big classes, but the figures are of different vintages, come from different definitions, and overlap. The pie is a "gross value of major asset classes" chart, **not** a net-worth pie. No number below was invented; derived numbers show their arithmetic.

## 4. Caveats (read before quoting any percentage)

1. **Double counting.** Broad money is mostly bank liabilities that fund loans, bonds and mortgages; equities and bonds are claims on the same corporate assets; real estate is partly financed by debt that also appears as bonds/deposits; private-market AUM includes private real estate/infrastructure that overlaps Savills; equities overlap with PE (IPOs/exits). The $912T is a sum of gross values, **not** net wealth.
2. **Debt is a claim, not wealth.** The $160.7T of bonds is the other side of someone's liability; net global wealth nets it out. UBS's $517.7T is net of household debt only.
3. **Different dates.** Real estate is end-2024; bonds/equities end-2025; money to mid-2026; gold/BTC mid-Oct 2026. Equities are up materially in 2026 (one secondary source, Ocorian, says ~$156.7T early Sep 2026 on a different base of $137.5T at end-2025, so I did not mix it in). WFE ($151.9T) and SIFMA ($157.8T) differ by coverage.
4. **Estimates, not measurements:** real estate (Savills modelling; China ~23.5%), private markets (PitchBook/Preqin/CFA range $18–20T, AUM ≠ market value), art (UHNW-only, forward projection), broad money (national definitions mixed; US M2 $23.3T vs IMF-style broad money $30.7T for the same country).
5. **Price dependence.** Gold (≈$30T at $4,182/oz; WGC's end-Q2 figure was $29.0T; Q1-average price would give ~$35T), BTC ($81.5K) and all crypto swing by ±10–20% in weeks. Bitcoin's share is 0.18% of the pie — about 5.5% of gold's value.
6. **What can't be measured well:** global M1, physical cash precisely, commodities stock value, silver, unlisted private businesses, collectibles outside the UHNW, non-UBS-sample wealth (~8%), government/corporate-owned real assets, derivatives (notional in the hundreds of trillions, not wealth), and Bitcoin lost/inaccessible coins.
7. Several figures here come via secondary aggregators (m2index, MacroMicro, FinObservatory, CoinMarketCap); primary-source numbers should be verified before publication.
8. Nothing here is investment advice.
